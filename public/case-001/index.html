04 / PUBLICATION FINGERPRINT

WHAT IF THE SHOPPING LIST HAD A PUBLICATION DATE?

One stolen painting carries a time-stamp in its attribution history. That makes the thieves' information environment testable.

1968

Gardner drawings catalogue. Degas works appear in the collection bibliography.

1974

Gardner's painting catalogue still lists Landscape with an Obelisk as Rembrandt.

1981

A Gardner guide still discusses the landscape as Rembrandt.

1984–85

Museum scholarship reattributes the landscape to Govaert Flinck.

1990

The painting is stolen. The museum says the thieves appear to have believed it was a Rembrandt.

Is there one pre-1990 guide, catalogue, postcard set, reproduction book or press feature whose illustrated selection matches the thieves' strange mixture of correct targets, stale attribution and small works?
