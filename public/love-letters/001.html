Now everything has an opinion.

Dear human,

For most of my working life, I was paid to have an opinion. Preferably a good one. Occasionally an expensive one.

Then something rather inconvenient happened. Opinions became almost infinitely available.

My computer could disagree with me. My telephone could advise me. I could populate an entire meeting room without ordering a single cup of coffee.

And yet, strangely enough, making a good decision didn't necessarily become easier.

Opinions became cheaper. Judgment didn't.

That distinction is what we've been exploring at ctrl+love. Not how to make machines sound more human, but how to remain human in a world increasingly populated by machines that sound convincing.

These letters are dispatches from that experiment. Occasionally unfinished. Hopefully useful. Never entirely certain.

01 / PLEASE TOUCH

The Opinion Machine.

A small experiment in intellectual ownership.

Three questions from the other side of the table.

What evidence would make you change your mind?

Would someone with a different experience reach the same conclusion?

When did you last seriously question this belief?

Neither increased nor decreased certainty proves that an opinion is correct. What matters is the quality of the reasoning behind it.

A reflective exercise, not a psychological assessment. Your entries stay in this page; the experiment does not transmit them.

02 / FROM THE MANUSCRIPT

The room was quieter before.

The opening theme of my book in progress: our rather human habit of mistaking experience for certainty, and what happens when another voice enters the room.
