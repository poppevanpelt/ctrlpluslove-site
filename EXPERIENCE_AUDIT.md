# ctrl+love Phase 2 Experience Audit

Creative review only. This assumes the site is technically sound and ignores code, SEO, and performance.

Reviewed through the combined lens of Steve Jobs, Dieter Rams, Rory Sutherland, Paula Scher, Rick Rubin, a Fortune 500 CMO, a first-time founder, a skeptical CEO, a journalist, and a creative director.

## Executive Summary

ctrl+love is close to being memorable because it has something rare: a business service with myth, objects, rituals, and a point of view. The strongest version of the brand is not a consultancy, not an agency, not a SaaS product, and not simply a creative network. It is a category of its own: a decision pressure institution.

The phrase "Shortcut to reality" is excellent. The Steel Ball is excellent. "Confidence should feel heavy" is excellent. The Museum Shop, AI-y-fier, Meeting Filter, and department posters make ctrl+love feel like a living world rather than a service website.

The risk is that the homepage and some commercial pages still explain the offer in conventional consulting language before visitors have fully felt the difference. The unforgettable parts are already present, but too many of them sit one layer deeper. The site should make the visitor think: "I have never seen a company like this, and somehow I trust it more because of that."

Overall creative diagnosis:

ctrl+love should become less like a site about decision rooms and more like entering a decision room.

## Brand Read

ctrl+love feels partly like a consultancy, partly like an agency, partly like a product, partly like a philosophy, and most interestingly like a category of its own.

It is not quite a consultancy because the language is too strange, too artifact-led, too authored.

It is not quite an agency because the promise is not "we make the thing"; it is "we pressure the decision before the thing meets reality."

It is not quite a product because the room is assembled, not subscribed to like software.

It is a movement only at the edges. The public has not yet been given rituals, badges, quotable beliefs, or a way to participate beyond contacting the company.

The ownable category:

Decision Rooms for reality contact.

Or sharper:

A decision pressure institution for leaders who need reality before reality arrives.

## Experience Principles For Phase 2

1. Lead with the object, then explain the system.
2. Make every page either a room, an artifact, a file, a poster, or a verdict.
3. Let the visitor touch the thinking, not just read about it.
4. Put Poppe at the exact moments where taste, judgment, and trust matter.
5. Turn commercial pages into acts of diagnosis, not menus.
6. Make every important page produce a sentence someone would quote tomorrow.
7. Make the brand feel discovered, not presented.

## Page Reviews

## Homepage

First Impression:

"Shortcut to reality" is instantly strong. In 10 seconds, visitors understand that ctrl+love stress-tests important decisions before market consequences arrive. They feel intelligence, restraint, pressure, and a little danger. The confusion is whether this is a consulting offer, a workshop, an AI system, or a branded creative practice. Curiosity comes from "Decision Rooms," "The Steel Ball," and the refusal of comfort.

Memorability:

The homepage is memorable in pieces, not yet as a single unforgettable journey. Someone may remember "Shortcut to reality" and "confidence should feel heavy." They may mention the Steel Ball. They are less likely to screenshot the process grids or anonymized project cards. The final line "Bring the decision into the room" is clear but repeated often enough to lose some electricity.

Emotional Journey:

Smile: Steel Ball, founder-editor language, "less theatre, more useful friction."

Hesitate: when "AI systems, expert judgment, market context" sounds like a familiar strategy offer.

Excited: when the site starts feeling like a system of rooms, artifacts, and pressure.

Lose momentum: anonymized proof cards and repeated explanatory sections.

Signature Moments:

Yes, but partially submerged. The Steel Ball section is the signature. The homepage needs one live "room moment" above the fold or immediately after it: a decision enters, three lenses disagree, a verdict changes.

Commercial Journey:

Curiosity becomes trust through founder credentials and the human network. Trust becomes action less naturally because the visitor is asked to choose among products before they have felt the diagnostic power. The chain breaks at proof: anonymized projects are credible but not vivid.

Founder Presence:

Poppe appears in the right area, but too late. The founder should not dominate the homepage, but should appear as host or editor earlier: one sentence that says why he built this after 30 years of watching expensive certainty.

## Lens Notes From The Review Board

Steve Jobs:

The Steel Ball and Necessary Elimination are the strongest. Simplify the homepage around one dramatic idea. Fewer explanations. More inevitability.

Dieter Rams:

The system is strongest when useful. Meeting Filter, pricing clarity, and Decision Artifact are good. Remove decorative cleverness that does not clarify.

Rory Sutherland:

The genius is in changing the perceived value of disagreement. Make "useful friction" feel desirable, scarce, and socially intelligent.

Paula Scher:

The department posters and document systems are brand theater with typographic authority. Make the whole site feel like a designed institution.

Rick Rubin:
