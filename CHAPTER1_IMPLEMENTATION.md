# Chapter 1 Implementation

Created: 2026-07-14

## What Changed

The homepage was rebuilt as the first public Room instead of a conventional company explanation.

The new Chapter 1 sequence is:

1. Arrival
2. The First Room
3. Final Verdict
4. Decision Artifact
5. Steel Ball
6. Constitution Article I
7. Admission

The previous homepage's broad explanatory flow, project proof, offer cards, founder biography, network preview, and practical FAQ were removed from the homepage experience. Those ideas still belong elsewhere in the institution, but they no longer lead the first public encounter.

## Why It Changed

The v2 blueprint and experience audit both identify the same issue: ctrl+love is most powerful when a visitor experiences judgment changing before they read what the company does.

The homepage now demonstrates the Room first. A decision enters with confidence, receives pressure from distinct perspectives, changes recommendation, produces a verdict, and leaves behind a Decision Artifact.

This supports the institution because it makes the Room the center of the world. The site no longer begins as a pitch. It begins as a place with rules.

## How The Changes Support The Institution

### Arrival

The first viewport is quiet and minimal: ctrl+love, institutional navigation, "Shortcut to Reality.", and Room status.

This creates curiosity without explaining the method too early. The visitor enters the institution before being sold to.

### The First Room

Decision 014 appears already in motion:

"Should we expand into Brazil next quarter?"

The decision starts with a recommendation to proceed and 82% confidence. Three perspectives then apply pressure: commercial quality, customer reality, and execution consequence.

This demonstrates the Room's institutional purpose: making internal confidence harder to fool.

### Final Verdict

The Room produces one outcome:

"Delay launch. Run six weeks of retention validation. Re-enter the Room."

The verdict is deliberately concise. It shows relief after tension: the decision is no longer vague, but it is also not falsely comfortable.

### Decision Artifact

The artifact turns the changed judgment into a printable institutional object. It records the original decision, pressure applied, blind spot, decision, confidence, and reality contact.

This makes the Room memorable and gives the institution a proof object instead of a sales claim.

### Steel Ball

Only the essential line remains on the homepage:

"Confidence should feel heavy."

The Steel Ball appears as Artifact 001, reinforcing that confidence is not decoration. It is something that should withstand pressure.

### Constitution

Only Article I is shown:

"Reality always gets the final vote."

This gives the Room governance without turning the homepage into a constitutional archive.

### Admission

The homepage ends with one question:

"What decision keeps you awake?"

The action is "Bring it into the Room." This keeps the CTA aligned with the institution's language and avoids generic contact-form behavior.

## Emotional Journey

Curiosity: the quiet arrival and Room status suggest something is already happening.

Observation: the visitor sees a real decision, confidence, recommendation, and status.

Tension: perspectives enter and confidence changes.

Relief: the final verdict gives one clear next move.

Trust: the Decision Artifact and Article I show that the institution has rules and records.

Need: the admission question turns the visitor back toward their own unresolved decision.

## What Was Intentionally Left Untouched

The Room page was not redesigned into "Watch the Room Think."

The Living Decision Review page was not elevated into the canonical proof experience.

Pricing was not rebuilt as Diagnosis.

Ambassadors were not converted into Around the Table seat files.

Museum, departments, AI-y-fier, Meeting Filter, and the broader Constitution were not reworked into the full institution map.

Poppe's founder-editor layer was not expanded on the homepage, because Chapter 1 should foreground the Room rather than the founder.

The lead-capture system was not rebuilt. Admission currently uses the existing email path so Chapter 1 stays focused on the first experience.

## Definition Of Done Check

The homepage should no longer feel like a company explaining itself.

It should feel like entering a quiet institution where a decision is already under pressure.
