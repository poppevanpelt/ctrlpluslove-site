# Savannah + 65: Vercel refund case trial

**Started:** 2026-11-03  
**Mode:** Read-only investigation  
**External actions taken:** None. No message sent, no new support case opened, no billing changes made.

## The objective

Pursue the user's existing Vercel refund request to a verified outcome, without requiring the user to keep personally carrying the case. The exact refund amount, invoice, and underlying charge rationale must be taken from the existing case and billing evidence; do not infer them.

## Evidence currently available

- Vercel's response says most refund requests may be self-resolved at `https://vercel.com/help`, and that a live support response may take 1–2 weeks.
- Vercel's Support Center documentation says customers can create and manage cases in the dashboard; if the AI support agent cannot resolve the issue, the documented route is to use **Create Case** and submit the proposed case form. Existing cases can be reopened from the Support tab when closed.
- Vercel's support guidance says billing@vercel.com is a direct channel for people unable to log in, people without a Vercel account who need to discuss invoices or unrecognized charges, and related billing-access situations. This is not evidence that email is the best route for every refund request.
- Public Vercel community answers repeatedly point billing-refund requests to the Help flow. These are corroborative examples, not a guarantee that a refund is eligible.

## First read of the response

The email is **not a refund denial**. It is a route back to self-service plus an estimated support queue time. It does not establish that the self-service route works for this particular invoice, that the existing request was correctly classified, or that the charge is refundable.

## Savannah + 65: first deliberation

Do not simulate 65 independent people all sending messages. Use 65 perspectives as a structured challenge to the current interpretation. Each perspective should contribute one of: a hypothesis, a missing piece of evidence, a legitimate alternative route, a risk, or a test that could change the next decision. Deduplicate the findings and keep one shared evidence ledger.

Initial lenses:
1. **Literalist:** What did Vercel actually say, and what did it not say?
2. **Customer advocate:** What outcome was requested, and has Vercel answered that request?
3. **Process mapper:** Is this already an open case, or only a self-service interaction?
4. **Billing investigator:** Which account, invoice, date, amount, and charge type are involved?
5. **Eligibility skeptic:** What published rule, if any, determines refund eligibility for this charge?
6. **Friction hunter:** Did the self-service path fail, omit the invoice, or merely redirect to support?
7. **Escalation strategist:** What specific new evidence would justify a higher-level review?
8. **Relationship keeper:** How can the request stay firm, factual, and easy for a human to act on?
9. **Evidence auditor:** Which claims are verified, user-reported, or still unknown?
10. **Outcome verifier:** What record will prove that the refund or another agreed remedy actually happened?

The remaining perspectives should challenge assumptions from different angles, not invent facts or duplicate messages. The shared objective outranks theatrical disagreement.

## Current unknowns

- Exact invoice and charge details.
- The wording and status of the existing support case beyond the latest reply.
- Whether self-service was attempted and, if so, what happened.
- Whether the request was explicitly stated as a refund request with the relevant invoice selected.
- Applicable eligibility policy for this specific charge.

## Next safe move

Continue read-only research against the existing case and official policy. Avoid opening a duplicate ticket while the current case may still be active. If the existing record shows that self-service failed or did not address the request, prepare a concise, evidence-backed escalation draft. Sending it requires a verified authorized channel and case-scoped permission.

## Stop conditions

- Do not describe a generic acknowledgement as a denial.
- Do not treat queue time alone as proof of a dead end.
- Do not invent invoice details or refund eligibility.
- Do not send duplicate or public messages merely to create pressure.
- Do not close the case until the objective is verified against evidence.
