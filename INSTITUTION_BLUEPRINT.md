# ctrl+love v2 — Experience Architecture Blueprint

Created: 2026-07-14  
Posture: creative, product, narrative, and institutional architecture.  
Scope: planning only. Do not begin broad implementation from this document without an explicit implementation request.

## 1. Vision

ctrl+love is a decision pressure institution.

It exists for the dangerous interval between internal confidence and external consequence: the moment when a team already has a plan, already has momentum, already has a deck, and is about to mistake alignment for truth.

The institution's job is not to improve brainstorming. It is not to make people feel creative. It is not a consultancy, agency, SaaS product, or AI wrapper. It is a room where important decisions are made harder to fool.

The public experience should feel less like reading a service website and more like entering a place with rules, artifacts, departments, rituals, and visible judgment. The visitor should encounter a decision under pressure before they encounter an explanation. They should watch perspectives disagree. They should feel confidence become heavier. They should leave with the sense that every consequential decision deserves a room before reality arrives.

The site already contains the raw material of an institution: Shortcut to Reality, the Decision Room, the Steel Ball, the Museum, Reality Preservation, Necessary Elimination, the Constitution, AI-y-fier, Meeting Filter, Decision Artifacts, ambassadors, and Poppe as founder-editor. Version 2 should stop treating these as separate clever pages and make them one world.

The desired memory is precise:

> I wish every important decision had one of those rooms.

Every future page, feature, artifact, and commercial path must make the Room feel more necessary. If it does not, it should not be built.

## 2. Narrative

The public journey should follow one emotional sequence.

### Act 1 — Enter the Room

The visitor should immediately face a decision, not a proposition.

The homepage becomes the first room. A decision enters with confidence. The visitor sees its assumptions, stakes, and initial recommendation. No preamble. No product menu. The institution starts by applying pressure.

Emotional state: curiosity with a little danger.

### Act 2 — Watch Judgment

The Room comes alive. Perspectives disagree. A confident recommendation starts to wobble. Commercial reality, human insight, evidence, reputation, execution, and culture all apply different pressure. The visitor understands the method emotionally before they understand the service model.

The Room page should stop behaving like a directory and become a live table. Personas and ambassadors become useful only when they can visibly change the decision.

Emotional state: "Oh, this is not another workshop."

### Act 3 — Receive the Artifact

The output is not a report. It is a Decision Artifact: concise, beautiful, printable, collectible, and slightly severe.

The artifact should make the change visible:

- the original decision,
- the pressure applied,
- the contradiction found,
- the confidence shift,
- what to protect,
- what to kill,
- what to make braver,
- the next move.

Emotional state: relief mixed with seriousness.

### Act 4 — Discover the Institution

Once the visitor has felt the Room, the wider world becomes legible. Museum, departments, Constitution, Steel Ball, Reality Preservation, Necessary Elimination, Meeting Filter, AI-y-fier, and artifacts all become institutional systems that protect reality contact.

They are not side quests. They are evidence that ctrl+love has a culture, not just an offer.

Emotional state: discovery.

### Act 5 — Meet the Editor

Poppe appears only where authorship creates trust. He is not the hero of the site. He is the founder-editor: the person who protects taste, pressure, rhythm, and reality contact.

His role is strongest in founder notes, constitutional annotations, pricing philosophy, field observations, and the transition from playful artifact to serious commercial consequence.

Emotional state: trust in the keeper of the room.

### Act 6 — Around the Table

Ambassadors are not profiles. They are seats.

The visitor should see why someone enters, what pressure they apply, what blind spot they catch, and how the decision changes because of them. The directory becomes a table. The table becomes a promise: the Room can invite the right reality into the decision before the market does.

Emotional state: "The right people would actually see what we miss."

### Act 7 — Leave Changed

The visitor exits with a new standard for decisions. The desired action is not merely "contact us." It is "bring the decision into the Room."

Emotional state: need.

## 5. Visitor Journey

### Minute 0

The visitor lands on a decision already in motion.

They see an initial recommendation, confidence level, and stakes. The first impression is not "what does this company do?" but "something is happening to this decision."

Desired thought: "I want to see what the Room does to it."

### Minute 1

The recommendation changes.

A perspective challenges the decision. Evidence enters. Confidence drops or becomes heavier. The visitor sees that disagreement is structured, useful, and consequential.

Desired thought: "This catches things a normal meeting would protect."

### Minute 3

The visitor receives or previews a Decision Artifact.

The artifact shows the original decision, revised decision, contradiction, confidence shift, and next move. It is beautiful enough to print and severe enough to discuss.

Desired thought: "This is not a report. This is a verdict I could take back to a team."

### Minute 10

The visitor explores the institution.

They find the Steel Ball, Museum, departments, Constitution, founder-editor notes, ambassadors, and pricing diagnosis. The world feels coherent because each piece explains how the institution protects reality contact.

Desired thought: "This is bigger than a service. It has rules."

### One week later

The visitor remembers one object and one need.

The object is probably the Steel Ball or a Decision Artifact. The need is the Room. The site has given them language to use internally: "We need to put this decision in the Room before it becomes expensive."

Desired thought: "I wish every important decision had one of those rooms."

## 7. Signature Moments

The v2 experience should create these unforgettable moments.

1. A decision enters the homepage as "Launch now" and leaves as "Proof sprint first."
2. Confidence becomes visibly heavier, not merely lower.
3. Perspectives disagree in public, with named pressure roles.
4. The Room changes the question, not just the answer.
5. A Decision Artifact appears as the receipt of judgment.
6. The Steel Ball becomes the physical symbol of reality contact.
7. Pricing starts with "What consequence are you trying to avoid?"
8. The Meeting Filter gives a copyable polite no.
9. AI-y-fier inflates language until Reality Preservation intervenes.
10. An ambassador takes a seat because a specific blind spot requires them.
11. A founder-editor note quietly explains why the rule exists.
12. The Constitution shows visible articles before the download.
13. The Museum object label reveals the decision lesson behind the joke.
14. Departments behave like operating systems, not posters.
15. The final CTA feels like admission: "Bring the decision into the Room."

## 8. Founder Role

Poppe's precise role is founder-editor.

He is the authorial keeper of the institution, not the protagonist. His job in the public experience is to make judgment feel protected by taste, experience, and responsibility.

He should appear where his presence adds trust:

- an early homepage editor sentence,
- the Inside ctrl+love founder note,
- Constitution annotations,
- pricing philosophy,
- Decision Review editor's note,
- field observations,
- selected artifact provenance,
- Around the Table host framing.

He should not appear where it would feel promotional:

- every CTA,
- every product card,
- every room perspective,
- every proof point,
- every museum object.

Voice rules:

- short,
- specific,
- observational,
- never self-congratulatory,
- never guru-like,
- always in service of reality contact.

Example role line:

> Founder-editor: curator of judgment, protector of reality contact, keeper of the Room's standards.

## 9. Category

ctrl+love is creating the category of the decision pressure institution.

Category statement:

> A decision pressure institution for leaders who need reality before reality arrives.

Supporting language:

- Decision Rooms for reality contact.
- Stress-test important decisions before the market does.
- Useful disagreement before expensive consequence.
- Confidence should feel heavy.
- Every important decision deserves a room.

The category should be explained through experience first and language second. Visitors should feel the category before they can name it.

## 10. Design Principles

1. Make the Room feel necessary.
2. Start with a decision, not an explanation.
3. Show judgment changing in public.
4. Make every output an artifact.
5. Treat confidence as physical, not decorative.
6. Let disagreement create trust.
7. Give every page an institutional role.
8. Keep the weirdness in the sales path.
9. Connect every joke to a serious consequence.
10. Use Poppe as editor, not mascot.
11. Turn people into seats around the table.
12. Turn pricing into diagnosis.
13. Turn proof into a visible before-and-after.
14. Make pages feel discovered, not presented.
15. Remove cleverness that does not protect reality contact.
16. Preserve the phrases that already carry the world: Shortcut to Reality, Steel Ball, Confidence should feel heavy, Decision Room, Reality Preservation, Necessary Elimination, The Constitution, AI-y-fier, Meeting Filter, founder-editor.
17. Never let the site collapse back into consultancy language when it could behave like an institution.
